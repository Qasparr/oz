### FALSEHOOD the second — the Virgil tag

Crowley's Prolegomena, §3, opens: *"As a weary but victorious warrior
delights to recall his battles — Fortisan hæc olim meminisse juvabit* —
we would linger for a moment upon the difficulties of our task."* The tag
is verbatim in 777 — as Crowley's tag, TRVVTH. Weighed **as a quotation
of Virgil**, it is FALSEHOOD.

The verified text of Virgil, *Aeneid* 1.203: **"Forsan et haec olim
meminisse iuvabit"** [verified against Macrobius, *Saturnalia*, and the
standard editions]. Crowley's form differs in three places: *Fortisan*
for *Forsan et* (Crowley's form matches neither classical *forsan* nor
*forsitan* — preserved [sic]); *hæc* for *haec* (orthographic); *juvabit*
for *iuvabit* (orthographic — in Latin, j is i). Crowley himself presents
it as his own tag with his own approximate translation ("[Lat. approx.
'perhaps it will be pleasant to remember these things one day.']"); he
nowhere claims it verbatim Virgil. The revision therefore preserves his
tag exactly as printed, marked [sic], and gives Virgil verbatim in the
footnote. The FALSEHOOD attaches to the *reading* of the tag as Virgil —
a reading the audit tested and the text does not support.
